# Phase 5 Stakeholder Interview Guide

**Purpose:** Open-ended interviews to validate problem hypotheses, understand current monitoring practices, and assess institutional fit

**Target Participants:** PhilHealth benefit development teams, implementation/monitoring teams, regional offices, DOH program managers

**Format:** Semi-structured 45-60 minute interviews (in-person or video call)

**Approach:** Exploratory and hypothesis-testing, not

 sales-oriented

---

## Interview Structure

### Total Duration: 45-60 minutes

1. **Introduction and Consent (3-5 minutes)**
2. **Current Practices and Challenges (15-20 minutes)**
3. **Information Needs and Gaps (10-15 minutes)**
4. **Tool and Dashboard Discussion (10-15 minutes)** [If MVP demonstration was conducted]
5. **Institutional Context and Adoption (10 minutes)**
6. **Wrap-Up and Next Steps (5 minutes)**

---

## Section 1: Introduction and Consent (3-5 minutes)

### Script

"Thank you for agreeing to speak with me today. I really appreciate your time.

**About this interview:**  
I'm researching how health benefit implementation is monitored—specifically, how teams track whether newly launched benefits are being delivered as intended. I'm exploring whether diagnostic tools could help identify where implementation is stalling.

**Your expertise:**  
You have valuable experience with [benefit development / implementation monitoring / regional operations], and I'd love to learn from your perspective.

**Format:**  
I'll ask you about your current monitoring practices, challenges you face, and information needs. There are no right or wrong answers—I'm here to learn, not evaluate.

**Confidentiality:**  
Your responses will be kept confidential. I may share anonymized themes from these interviews (e.g., "several participants mentioned..."), but I won't attribute specific quotes to you without permission.

**Recording:**  
[If applicable] Would you be comfortable if I record this conversation for note-taking purposes? The recording would only be used internally and not shared.

**Questions:**  
Do you have any questions before we begin? Are you comfortable proceeding?"

### Key Points
- Frame as research/learning, not sales
- Emphasize confidentiality
- Obtain verbal consent
- Invite questions

---

## Section 2: Current Practices and Challenges (15-20 minutes)

### Goal: Understand how participants currently monitor benefit implementation and what challenges they face

---

**Question 1: Benefit Monitoring Process**

"Can you walk me through how you currently monitor the implementation of a new health benefit after it's launched?"

**Probes:**
- What stages or milestones do you track? (e.g., facility accreditation, claims volumes, payments)
- How frequently do you review implementation status? (daily, weekly, monthly, quarterly)
- What data sources do you use? (reports, dashboards, spreadsheets, meetings)
- Who generates the data or reports? (your team, IT, analytics, external vendor)

**Listening For:**
- Existing monitoring infrastructure (dashboards, reports, data systems)
- Frequency and depth of monitoring
- Data sources and data quality
- Division of responsibility (who monitors what)

---

**Question 2: Common Implementation Challenges**

"What are the most common challenges you encounter when implementing a new benefit package?"

**Probes:**
- Do certain problems occur repeatedly across different benefits? (e.g., low facility participation, payment delays)
- Which stage of implementation tends to be most problematic? (accreditation, claims filing, approval, payment)
- Are there regional variations in implementation challenges?
- How do you typically become aware that implementation is not going as planned?

**Listening For:**
- Recurring bottlenecks (validates problem hypotheses)
- Severity and frequency of problems
- Problem detection methods (reactive vs proactive)
- Regional or contextual variations

---

**Question 3: Bottleneck Identification**

"When implementation is slower than expected or not working well, how do you identify where the bottleneck is?"

**Probes:**
- What information do you look at first?
- Do you have standardized methods or criteria for diagnosing problems?
- How do you distinguish between different types of bottlenecks? (e.g., accreditation barriers vs reimbursement delays)
- How long does it typically take to pinpoint the root cause?

**Listening For:**
- Diagnostic methods (systematic vs ad hoc)
- Information sources for diagnosis
- Time and effort required for diagnosis
- Confidence in identifying root causes

---

**Question 4: Existing Tools and Reports**

"What existing dashboards, reports, or monitoring tools do you currently use for benefit implementation tracking?"

**Probes:**
- Can you describe what information these tools provide?
- What do you find most useful about them?
- What's frustrating or missing from these tools?
- Do different teams have access to different tools or data?
- Are there metrics you wish you could track but currently can't?

**Listening For:**
- Inventory of existing tools (competitive landscape)
- Functionality gaps (unmet needs)
- Data silos (lack of integration)
- User satisfaction with current tools

---

**Question 5: Decision-Making Based on Monitoring Data**

"Can you give me an example of a time when monitoring data led you to take a specific action?"

**Probes:**
- What did the data show?
- What decision did you make based on that information?
- Who else was involved in the decision?
- What additional information did you wish you had?
- What was the outcome of your action?

**Listening For:**
- How monitoring data informs decisions
- Decision-making authority and processes
- Information gaps that hinder decisions
- Real-world use cases for diagnostic tools

---

**Question 6: Reimbursement and Payment Challenges** [For non-finance roles]

"In your experience, how common are reimbursement or payment delays for benefit claims?"

**Probes:**
- Do facilities complain about payment delays? How often?
- What typically causes payment delays? (budget constraints, documentation issues, banking problems)
- How visible is payment status in your current monitoring? (Can you easily see which facilities have been paid?)
- Do payment delays affect provider participation or satisfaction?

**Listening For:**
- Severity of reimbursement bottlenecks (validates problem hypothesis)
- Visibility of payment data (information gap)
- Consequences of payment delays (impact on implementation)

---

## Section 3: Information Needs and Gaps (10-15 minutes)

### Goal: Identify unmet information needs and validate whether Finvanta's value proposition resonates

---

**Question 7: Ideal Information**

"If you could have any additional information about benefit implementation that you don't currently have access to, what would it be?"

**Probes:**
- What questions do you wish you could answer more easily?
- What would help you make faster or better implementation decisions?
- Are there metrics you track manually that should be automated?
- What visibility do you lack across different parts of the implementation process? (facilities, claims, payments, regions)

**Listening For:**
- Explicit information needs (feature requests)
- Pain points with current tools
- Desired metrics or analyses
- Integration needs (cross-functional visibility)

---

**Question 8: Integrated View of Implementation**

"Currently, do you have a single place where you can see facility participation, claims processing, and reimbursement status together?"

**Probes:**
- If no: Would an integrated view be useful? Why or why not?
- If yes: What tool provides this? What works well and what doesn't?
- How much time do you spend pulling together data from different sources?
- Do different teams (accreditation, claims, finance) share data easily or is it siloed?

**Listening For:**
- Data silos and integration needs (validates value proposition)
- Workarounds for lack of integration (manual effort)
- Cross-functional visibility gaps

---

**Question 9: Regional Disparities**

"How important is it for you to compare regional performance when monitoring benefit implementation?"

**Probes:**
- Do you currently have easy access to regional comparisons?
- What regional variations have you observed in past benefit rollouts?
- How do you decide which regions need additional support or resources?
- Are regional disparities typically due to local capacity, policy differences, or other factors?

**Listening For:**
- Importance of regional analysis (validates feature relevance)
- Existing regional monitoring practices
- Explanations for regional variations (contextual factors)

---

**Question 10: Diagnostic Recommendations**

"If a dashboard could automatically flag implementation bottlenecks and recommend areas for investigation, would that be useful?"

**Probes:**
- What would make a recommendation credible or actionable?
- How would you want recommendations prioritized? (severity, urgency, feasibility)
- Would you trust automated recommendations, or would you prefer to interpret data yourself?
- What thresholds or benchmarks would you use to define a 'bottleneck'?

**Listening For:**
- Receptiveness to automated diagnostics
- Credibility requirements (validation needs)
- Preferred recommendation format (prescriptive vs suggestive)
- Threshold calibration needs

---

## Section 4: Tool and Dashboard Discussion (10-15 minutes)

### [If participant has seen MVP demonstration, use this section. If not, skip to Section 5.]

---

**Question 11: Overall Impression**

"Now that you've seen the dashboard demonstration, what's your overall impression? Would this type of tool be useful in your work?"

**Probes:**
- What did you find most useful or interesting?
- What was confusing or unclear?
- How does this compare to tools you currently use?
- Would this address an information gap you experience, or does it duplicate existing tools?

**Listening For:**
- Perceived usefulness (validates value proposition)
- Competitive positioning (differentiation vs duplication)
- Clarity of presentation (user comprehension)

---

**Question 12: Specific Use Cases**

"Can you describe a specific situation in which you would use this dashboard?"

**Probes:**
- What decisions would it help you make?
- How frequently would you use it? (daily, weekly, monthly, ad hoc)
- Who else would you share insights with? (colleagues, leadership, regional offices)
- What would you do after identifying a bottleneck with the dashboard?

**Listening For:**
- Concrete use cases (validates product-market fit)
- Usage frequency (adoption potential)
- Sharing and collaboration needs (social features)
- Action pathways (how insights translate to decisions)

---

**Question 13: Missing Features**

"What's missing from the dashboard that would make it more useful for you?"

**Probes:**
- Are there metrics or indicators not currently shown that you'd want to see?
- Would you want facility-level drill-down, or is national/regional sufficient?
- Would you want historical trend analysis or time-series comparisons?
- Would you want to customize thresholds or filters?

**Listening For:**
- Feature requests (product roadmap priorities)
- Depth of analysis needed (national, regional, facility-level)
- Customization needs (user preferences vary)

---

**Question 14: Credibility of Recommendations**

"Were the diagnostic recommendations credible? Did the severity levels (critical, moderate) seem appropriate?"

**Probes:**
- Were the thresholds reasonable? (e.g., 70% disbursement target, 50 facilities without payment)
- Did the recommendations tell you something you didn't already know, or confirm what you suspected?
- Would you trust these recommendations enough to escalate to leadership or take action?
- What would increase your confidence in the recommendations?

**Listening For:**
- Perceived credibility (validates recommendation engine)
- Threshold calibration feedback (refinement needs)
- Novelty vs confirmation of known issues
- Trust requirements (validation, transparency)

## Section 5: Institutional Context and Adoption (10 minutes)

### Goal: Assess institutional barriers, data access feasibility, and adoption potential

---

**Question 15: Data Access and Governance**

"If this tool were refined and we wanted to use real PhilHealth data, what would be required to obtain data access?"

**Probes:**
- Who would need to approve data access? (IT, legal, leadership, data governance committee)
- What data privacy or security requirements would apply?
- How long does the data approval process typically take?
- Would data need to be anonymized or aggregated?
- Are there precedents for external tools accessing PhilHealth administrative data?

**Listening For:**
- Data governance processes (access pathway)
- Timeline for data access (feasibility)
- Privacy and security requirements (compliance needs)
- Institutional openness to external tools

---

**Question 16: Institutional Barriers**

"What barriers do you foresee to adopting a tool like this at PhilHealth?"

**Probes:**
- Organizational priorities: Is benefit monitoring a priority, or are there competing initiatives?
- Resource constraints: Do teams have capacity to adopt new tools?
- Tool fatigue: Are teams already overwhelmed with dashboards and reporting tools?
- Procurement processes: Would this require formal procurement or vendor approval?
- Change management: How receptive is the organization to new monitoring approaches?

**Listening For:**
- Adoption barriers (risk assessment)
- Organizational priorities (timing considerations)
- Tool landscape (competition and saturation)
- Procurement complexity (go-to-market considerations)

---

**Question 17: Pilot Interest**

"If we refined this tool based on feedback and it used real PhilHealth data, would you be interested in piloting it?"

**Probes:**
- Under what conditions would you pilot this? (data quality, feature completeness, approval from leadership)
- What would a successful pilot look like to you? (outcomes, duration, scope)
- Who else would need to be involved in a pilot decision?
- What concerns would you want addressed before piloting?

**Listening For:**
- Willingness to pilot (adoption signal)
- Pilot readiness requirements (prerequisites)
- Decision-making process (stakeholder map)
- Risk tolerance (pilot scope and commitments)

---

**Question 18: Institutional Champion**

"Do you know of anyone at PhilHealth or DOH who might be particularly interested in this type of tool or who advocates for better benefit monitoring?"

**Probes:**
- Are there specific teams or individuals who are frustrated with current monitoring tools?
- Who has authority or influence over monitoring infrastructure decisions?
- Are there ongoing initiatives or projects that this tool might complement?

**Listening For:**
- Potential champions (referrals)
- Influential stakeholders (partnership opportunities)
- Complementary initiatives (collaboration opportunities)

---

## Interview Best Practices

### Preparation

1. **Research Participant Background:**
   - Review LinkedIn profile, PhilHealth org chart, recent publications
   - Understand their role, responsibilities, and likely pain points
   - Tailor questions to their context

2. **Review Previous Interviews:**
   - Identify themes emerging across participants
   - Prepare follow-up questions to test or refine themes
   - Avoid repetitive questions if prior participants already answered

3. **Test Technology:**
   - Ensure video conferencing link works
   - Test recording software (if applicable)
   - Have backup plan (phone number) if video fails

### During the Interview

1. **Build Rapport:**
   - Start with casual conversation
   - Express genuine interest in participant's work
   - Use warm, conversational tone (not interrogative)

2. **Listen Actively:**
   - Don't interrupt
   - Use verbal affirmations ("mm-hmm," "I see," "that's interesting")
   - Paraphrase to confirm understanding

3. **Probe Deeply:**
   - Ask "Why?" and "Can you tell me more about that?"
   - Request specific examples, not generalizations
   - Follow interesting threads even if not on script

4. **Stay Neutral:**
   - Don't defend design decisions
   - Don't lead participants to desired answers
   - Accept negative feedback graciously

5. **Manage Time:**
   - Keep track of time (45-60 minutes total)
   - Prioritize most important questions if running long
   - Respectfully wrap up if participant has hard stop

### After the Interview

1. **Document Immediately:**
   - Fill out interview template while memory is fresh
   - Capture quotes verbatim
   - Note non-verbal cues (tone, hesitation, enthusiasm)

2. **Reflect on Insights:**
   - What surprised you?
   - What confirmed prior hypotheses?
   - What questions remain unanswered?

3. **Update Research Questions:**
   - Refine questions for future interviews based on learnings
   - Identify gaps in understanding to address

## Synthesis Approach (After All Interviews)

### Step 1: Thematic Analysis

**Group findings by theme:**
- Current monitoring practices
- Implementation challenges and bottlenecks
- Information gaps and unmet needs
- Existing tools (inventory and satisfaction)
- Dashboard usefulness and credibility
- Missing features and requests
- Institutional barriers to adoption
- Pilot interest and conditions

### Step 2: Quantify Where Possible

- X of Y participants confirmed [problem/need]
- X of Y participants rated dashboard as useful (≥4/5)
- X of Y participants expressed pilot interest
- X of Y participants mentioned [specific bottleneck]

### Step 3: Identify Patterns

- Consistent themes across participants (validated hypotheses)
- Divergent perspectives (explore why)
- Role-specific needs (benefit teams vs regional offices)
- Surprising or unexpected findings

### Step 4: Decision-Making

- Is there a real problem Finvanta can solve? (problem-solution fit)
- Does dashboard address unmet needs? (product-market fit)
- Are participants willing to pilot? (adoption potential)
- What barriers must be overcome? (risk assessment)
